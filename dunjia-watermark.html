How to read the match strength

The match strength shows how clearly your key’s hidden pattern stands out in the image. Higher means a clearer match.

0% means no sign of the pattern, 50% is the point where the mark counts as found, and 100% is a very clear match.

A result of 50% or higher means “found”, and anything below 50% means “not found”.

The percentage is not a statistical probability. It does not mean “X% chance this image is yours” or the chance of a false match. It is a readable gauge of how strongly your key’s pattern shows up. The scale comes from simulated tests, so treat it as a rough guide, not a calibrated measurement.

A higher percentage comes from a stronger embed setting, larger images and smoother pictures. Heavy compression, resizing and editing lower it. A low result doesn’t prove the mark is absent, only that the evidence is weaker.

What this can and can’t tell you

This check reads the data stored inside the image file, such as Content Credentials (C2PA), IPTC labels and settings saved by AI tools. If the creator or tool labeled the image as AI-generated, this finds the label.

It does not look at the picture itself, so it can’t tell by appearance whether something is AI-made. Screenshots, social media uploads and re-saving usually strip this data, so “no markers found” does not mean the image is human-made. Invisible AI watermarks, such as Google’s SynthID, can’t be read by this app. Treat any result as one clue, not proof.

What this does and doesn’t do. The mark is applied across the entire image and may persist after JPEG re-saving and cropping. However, it is not foolproof and may be compromised by significant resizing, screenshots taken at different scales, AI regeneration, or deliberate removal. AI models may not recognize or respond to the embedded pattern, and the mark does not prevent an image from being copied, used, or processed by AI systems.
